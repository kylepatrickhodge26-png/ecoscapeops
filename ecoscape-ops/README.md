# EcoScape Ops

Multi-tenant operations app for landscaping businesses: Next.js (App Router) on Supabase (Postgres, Auth, row-level security).

**Built so far:** the multi-tenant foundation (business signup and login, strict per-business data isolation), **Customers** (add, edit, delete, list), **Scheduling** (recurring bookings, calendar and list views, job statuses), **Crew** (crew logins, job assignment, each crew member's own job view), the **Dashboard** (the home page), **Expenses** (a categorized expense log feeding the dashboard's profit), **Weather** (a live rain forecast for the service area, moving a rainy day's visits, and a ready-to-send text for each opted-in customer), **Invoicing** (invoices from visits and other items, a pay link offering each business's own payment apps, and payments recorded by the owner), **Route ordering** (each day's visits in driving order from the shop, per crew member, with arrows to change it), **Phone install** (add it to a phone's home screen like an app), **Quotes**, **Job photos and checklists**, **Reports and CSV export**, a public **Request form**, **Monthly invoices**, **Time tracking**, a **Today** screen with a one-tap **workday clock and job clock**, an owner **search box**, a **Help** page, a **customer FAQ**, and **Done for the season** (pausing a customer's repeat visits).

## Product decisions in this version

The customer fields come from the Add Customer form in the prototype (`../EcoScape Ops.html`), as agreed in place of `SPEC.md`, which isn't in the repo.

| Field | Notes |
| --- | --- |
| First name, last name, phone, email | At least one of these is required so every customer can be identified. The list shows name, else phone, else email, the same as the prototype. |
| Property address, billing address | Free text. An empty billing address shows as "Same as property". |
| Gate / access instructions, service notes | Free text. |
| Preferred day | Monday–Saturday. Defaults to Monday, like the prototype. |
| Status | Active / Inactive. |
| Notification preference | Text / Email. |
| SMS opt-in | Checkbox. |
| Customer since | Defaults to today and can be edited, for importing existing customers. |

Also:

- **Not built yet:** *Payment status* is left out because billing should work it out. *Services* (name, price, frequency) wait for scheduling.
- **Duplicate phone numbers:** as in the prototype, adding a customer whose phone number is already on file asks "could this be the same customer?". The owner can add them anyway. Formatting is ignored, so `(631) 555-0142` matches `631.555.0142`.
- **Delete** is permanent and asks for confirmation first. Setting a customer to *Inactive* is the way to keep their record.
- **One business per login** for now. This is one database constraint and can be relaxed later.
- **Roles:** memberships have a role, `owner` or `crew`. Only owners can read or change customers. What crew members can see will be decided when crew features are built.

## Scheduling decisions

Scheduling follows the prototype's Schedule screen, since `SPEC.md` isn't in the repo.

- **Booking** a service picks a customer, service, price, frequency and first visit. The first visit defaults to the customer's preferred day. Frequencies are weekly, every 2 weeks, every 3 weeks, or one time.
- **Visits are generated by the database.** A recurring booking creates the next 6 visits and keeps 6 on the schedule: completing or cancelling a visit adds the next one. The cadence stays anchored to the first visit, so rescheduling one visit doesn't shift the rest. A one-time booking creates a single visit.
- **Stopping** a recurring service (on the customer's page) stops new visits and removes its upcoming visits that haven't started. Past and in-progress visits stay.
- **Statuses:** scheduled, assigned, en route, in progress, completed, unable to complete, weather delay, cancelled. Each has its own shape *and* color (circle, ring, arrow, half circle, square, triangle, diamond, cross), plus a text label everywhere except the phone calendar's shapes.
- **List filters:**
  - *Upcoming*: today or later, and not completed or cancelled.
  - *Today*: visits dated today, whatever their status.
  - *Completed*.
  - *Needs attention*: unable to complete, weather delay, or **overdue** (a past visit never completed or cancelled).
  - *All*.
- **Could not service** asks for an optional reason, then either reschedules the visit (default: the next day) or cancels it. The reason and outcome are added to the visit's notes.
- **"Today"** uses the business's time zone, which is taken from the owner's browser at signup. It falls back to America/New_York.
- **Not built yet:** crew assignment (the *assigned* status exists but has no crew field), route ordering, weather ("move a day"), invoicing, and editing a service's price or frequency after booking. Individual visits can be edited.

## Crew decisions

- **Roles.** The *owner* has full access. A *crew* member sees and works only the jobs assigned to them.
- **Crew logins via invite links.** On the Crew page the owner adds a name and gets a private link to text the person.
  - The crew member opens it and creates their own email and password, which ties the login to the business.
  - A link works once and expires after 14 days. The owner can make a new one, which kills the old one.
  - Only a hash of the link is stored.
  - A login can belong to one business, so someone who already owns a business needs a separate login to join a crew.
- **The owner is on the crew too**, so jobs can be assigned to them. "Assign to" (on booking and on editing a visit) appears only once the business has **more than one** crew member, counting pending invites. Solo operators never see it. Booking a recurring service with a crew member assigns every visit, including ones added later.
- **What crew members see** (My jobs: overdue, today, the next 14 days):
  - customer name, address, phone, gate/access instructions, service notes, the service, and the job's notes;
  - **never prices**, other customers, or anyone else's jobs.
- **What crew members can do:** start a job, mark it completed, add a note, and *Could not service* (reschedule to a new date, or cancel). Their notes are signed with their name. They can't reach the owner's pages.
- **Removing** a crew member ends their access immediately and unassigns their jobs. Their login still exists but belongs to no business.
- **Not built yet:** route ordering and maps, weather, billing, reassigning a whole recurring service at once (visits can be reassigned one by one), and crew seeing each other.

## Dashboard decisions

- **The dashboard is everyone's home page, called Today.** Signup, sign-in and `/` land there. Owners see the whole business; crew members see only their own jobs. It opens with the person's own clock and route (see *Today and the clock*); the numbers below are for owners.
- **Job counts:** *Today* (with completed / remaining), *Tomorrow*, and *This week*, which means today plus the next 6 days. Cancelled visits never count. All dates use the business's time zone.
- **Revenue (owners only).** The headline is every non-cancelled visit booked this month at its price, completed or not. Most small operators don't invoice every visit, so counting only invoiced-and-paid money would understate it, which was the prototype's original mistake. A second line shows how much of that is already completed.
- **Estimated profit (owners only)** is this month's revenue (as above) minus this month's logged expenses.
- **Invoice totals:** *Outstanding* (owed on sent invoices, with how much is overdue) and money *collected this month*; see Invoicing decisions for how invoiced visits count toward revenue.
- **Quick actions (owners):** add a customer, book a job, **log gas**, **log equipment**, add any other expense, manage crew, and see what needs attention.
- **Today's jobs:** owners get the business's list, with the money cards at the bottom of the page. Crew members get only their own route (see *Today and the clock*), and never see prices or money totals.
- All the numbers come from one database function, `dashboard_summary()`, which scopes by role. A crew member's numbers can't include anyone else's jobs, and money totals are never sent to crew at all.

## Expenses decisions

- **Owner only.** Crew members can't see, log, change or delete expenses, and neither can anyone outside the business. The database enforces this.
- **Fields** follow the prototype: date, category, amount, vendor (optional) and notes (optional).
- **Categories:** fuel, equipment, repairs, materials, fertilizer, mulch, payroll, insurance, advertising, vehicle, other.
- **Quick-log on the dashboard.** *Log gas* and *Log equipment* open a small form right on the dashboard: amount, plus where it was bought if you like. The expense is logged for today. *+ Other expense* opens the full form.
- **Expenses page:** one month at a time, with that month's total, totals by category, and every entry. Entries can be edited and deleted.
- **Dashboard profit:** this month's booked revenue minus this month's expenses, in the business's time zone.
- Each expense records who logged it; that can't be faked or changed.

## Weather decisions

- **Forecast: the National Weather Service** (api.weather.gov). Free, with no account, key or card, so there's nothing to set up. It covers the US. The service-area ZIP code is turned into a map position with a ZIP code list bundled with the app (the `zipcodes` package), and each area's forecast is cached for an hour. (OpenWeather was used first; it's free too but asks for a card at signup.)
- **Service area:** a US ZIP code, set by the owner on the Weather page and shown as its town (e.g. "Ronkonkoma, NY"). Crew members see the forecast on their dashboard but can't change the area.
- **Dashboard card** (like the prototype's WEATHER card): the highest chance of rain among today, tomorrow and the day after, and which day it is. Owners also get *Move jobs & text customers →*. If the forecast is down, the card says so and the rest of the dashboard still works.
- **Rain day = "Move a day's jobs"** (from the prototype). The owner picks a day and a new day. Every visit that day that isn't completed or cancelled moves, is marked *Weather delay*, gets a "Moved from … due to weather" note, and keeps its crew assignment. Both days must be today or later.
- **Texting the affected customers, from the owner's own phone.** After a move, the rain delay's page lists each affected customer with their own message, for example: "Hi Jane, this is Acme Lawn Care. Due to the weather, we're moving your Tue, Sep 29 visit to Wed, Sep 30. Thanks! Reply STOP to opt out." (Plain characters and under 160, so it's one SMS segment in most cases.) *Open text* opens it in the phone's Messages app with the number and message filled in; the owner taps send. *Copy message* is there for sending from a computer. There's no texting service, so nothing to pay for or register. (A version that sent through Twilio automatically was built first and dropped because Twilio costs money; it's in the git history.)
- **Only opted-in customers ever get a text.** The database decides who can be texted: the business's own customers with a visit in that rain delay, SMS opt-in on, and a phone number that's a usable mobile number. Only they get a message and an *Open text* button. Everyone else is listed with the reason (not opted in / no usable number) so the owner can call them.
- **Keeping track.** The page shows which customers' texts have been opened ("2 of 5 opened") and when, so the owner can work down the list. The app can't see whether the text was actually sent from the phone.
- **STOP replies** arrive on the owner's phone like any other text. Turning off that customer's SMS opt-in removes their text from every rain delay page.
- SMS opt-in is the only switch that matters for texting; the separate *notification preference* (text/email) isn't used yet, as in the prototype.

## Invoicing decisions

- **Invoices** follow the prototype: numbered from #1001 per business, due in 14 days by default, for one customer. Lines can bill the customer's visits (picked from a list; the price can be changed on the invoice) and/or anything else ("Mulch, 2 × $30"). Optional notes for the customer. No sales tax.
- **Statuses:** *Draft* → *Sent* (or *Cancelled*) are set by the owner. *Partly paid*, *Paid* and *Overdue* are worked out from the payments and the due date every time an invoice is shown, so they can never be out of date. Overdue beats partly paid; paid beats overdue.
- **Drafts** can be edited or deleted. A **sent** invoice can't be edited (so what the customer saw is what's recorded); it can get a later due date, or be cancelled if nothing has been paid on it.
- **A visit can be on only one live invoice.** Cancelling an invoice frees its visits.
- **Sending:** each invoice has a private pay link. The owner shares it with *Open text* (from their own phone, like weather texts, and only for customers who opted in to texts), *Copy link* (to email it), or *Mark as sent* (e.g. a paper invoice). Any of these marks a draft sent. Drafts and cancelled invoices' links show nothing.
- **Auto-invoice (optional, off by default):** when a visit is marked completed, by the owner or a crew member, a draft invoice is created for it (unless it's already invoiced or free). The owner reviews and sends it.
- **How customers pay: each business's own payment apps** (Invoices → *Set up payment options*). The owner enters whichever they use: a Venmo username, Cash App $cashtag, PayPal.Me name, Zelle email or phone, and/or a payment link of their own (e.g. a Square link), plus a note (e.g. who to make checks out to). The invoice's pay link shows a *Pay with Venmo* / *Cash App* / *PayPal* button that opens the app with the balance filled in, who to send Zelle to (with a Copy button), and a *Pay online* button for the business's own link. The money goes straight from the customer to the business: there's no payment company in the middle, nothing for EcoScape Ops to sign up for, no fees, and no card or bank details ever reach the app. (Stripe was built first and taken out: it needed a Stripe account and keys.)
- **Payments are recorded by the owner** when the money arrives: cash, check, Venmo, Cash App, PayPal, Zelle, card, bank transfer or other, up to the balance due, with the date received and an optional note. Invoices don't mark themselves paid (the payment apps don't tell the app), so the pay link says the invoice shows as paid once the business has received the money. A payment recorded by mistake can be removed. **A payment is recorded once:** each form submission carries an id, so submitting it twice (or at the same moment) records one payment.
- **Dashboard revenue, without double counting:** each visit counts once, at its invoiced amount once it's on a sent invoice, otherwise at its booked price (in the month of the visit). Invoice lines that aren't visits count in the month the invoice was issued. Drafts and cancelled invoices don't change the numbers. Profit is that revenue minus expenses.
- **Owner only.** Crew members never see invoices, payments or money, even for the visits they did.

## Route ordering decisions

- **Shop address** (Settings, owner only): where routes start. It has to name its own town and state. It's looked up once and must be in the US, and within 50 miles of the weather service area if one is set. Its matched address is shown so the owner can check it.
- **Geocoder: the U.S. Census Bureau geocoder** (geocoding.geo.census.gov). Free, with no account, key or card. It has no published per-address limit (bulk files take up to 10,000 addresses; the app looks up one at a time, three at once at most). It only knows US addresses, so a match in another country can't come back from it in the first place. It's government data, so it can be stored. If it's down, addresses stay "waiting" and are tried again later; nothing breaks.
- **Customer addresses are looked up when they're saved** and the result is cached on the customer, so they're never looked up again unless the property address (or the shop) changes. The database resets a customer to "pending" whenever that happens, so a stale location is never used. Customers still waiting are looked up when their day's routes are opened, or with *Look up waiting addresses* in Settings.
- **Anchoring, so a bare street can't match the wrong place:** a bare street ("12 Oak St") is always looked up with the shop's town and state added. An address that names its own town or ZIP keeps it, so a customer in the next town over is still found; the shop's state is added if it has none. The country is always the US.
- **Flagged, never quietly accepted:** a match outside the US, or more than **50 miles** (straight line) from the shop, is flagged. The customer page and Settings say what it matched and how far away, and the visit goes last on the route marked *Location not found*. The same happens for addresses that can't be found. Fixing the address looks it up again.
- **Routes:** one per crew member per day (the owner is a crew member too), plus one for unassigned visits. The owner sees them all on the day's page (Schedule → a day); a crew member sees their own today under *My jobs*, and only their own visits. Cancelled visits aren't in routes; completed ones keep their place.
- **Suggested order:** nearest stop first from the start, then the nearest to that, and so on (nearest neighbor), using straight-line distance. It's worked out fresh each time, so it always reflects the day's current visits.
- **The arrows** (owner, or the crew member on their own route) override it, and the override is kept for that route and day. Visits added to the day afterwards go at the end. *Reset to suggested order* drops the override.
- **A different start for one day** (*Start somewhere else this day*): changes only that route on that day; the shop address stays as it is. It's looked up like a customer address (anchored, US only, within 50 miles of the shop). *Start from the shop* undoes it.

## Phone install decisions

- **Installable app (PWA).** The app has a web app manifest, icons and a small service worker, so it can be added to a phone's home screen and opens full screen like an app. No account, store or fee is involved.
  - **iPhone:** open the site in Safari, tap Share, then *Add to Home Screen*.
  - **Android:** open the site in Chrome, tap the menu, then *Install app* (or *Add to Home screen*).
- **Nothing private is stored on the phone by the worker.** `public/sw.js` caches exactly one thing, a fixed "You're offline" page (`public/offline.html`), and only shows it when a page can't load. It never caches pages, API calls, photos or customer data, so one person's data can't be served to another from a shared cache, and a signed-out phone shows nothing from before. Everything else always comes from the network, so the app needs a connection to show jobs.
- The worker, manifest and offline page are served without signing in (they hold no data). The worker is only registered in production builds, and `/sw.js` is never cached.
- Tests (`src/lib/pwa.test.ts`) fail if the worker ever starts caching anything beyond the offline page.

## Quotes decisions

- **Owner only.** Crew members never see quotes, lines or totals, and neither does anyone outside the business. Owners read quotes through row-level security; every change goes through a database function (`save_quote`, `send_quote`, `decline_quote`, `duplicate_quote`, `delete_draft_quote`, `accept_quote`) that checks the caller owns the business. Nobody writes quotes directly.
- **For a customer or a prospect.** A quote is either for an existing customer or for a *prospect* (name, phone, email, address) who isn't a customer yet. A prospect becomes a customer when the quote is accepted.
- **Fields:** a short title ("Spring cleanup"), lines (description, quantity, price), notes, and a *good through* date (30 days by default). Numbered per business (`Q-1001`, `Q-1002`, …). The total can't be more than $99,999.99, because an accepted quote becomes a service and that is the most a service can cost.
- **Statuses:** *Draft* → *Sent* → *Accepted* or *Declined*. *Expired* isn't stored: a sent quote past its good-through date shows as expired, worked out every time it's shown. A draft never shows as expired.
- **Only drafts can be changed.** To revise a sent or declined quote, *Copy to a new draft*. A draft can be deleted.
- **Sending:** *Copy as text* gives a plain-text message to paste into a text or email; *Open text* (only for existing customers who opted in to texts, like invoices and weather texts) opens it in the phone's Messages app; *Mark as sent* is for handing it over in person. Any of these marks a draft sent. *Print* gives a clean printable page (the menu and buttons are hidden).
- **Accepting books the work.** *They said yes* asks for the service name (the title by default), how often (one time by default), the first visit (today or later) and, with a crew, who it's assigned to. It books a service at the quote's total using the normal scheduling rules, so a recurring service gets its visits like any other. The quote is marked accepted and linked to the service. Accepting is allowed from any state except already accepted, so a customer who changes their mind can still be booked. **Accepting twice books once:** the second try finds it already accepted.
- Quotes don't count toward dashboard revenue until they become a booked service.

## Checklists and photos decisions

- **Checklists per service.** In *Settings → Checklists & photos* the owner writes a checklist per service name ("Mow, edge, blow, bag clippings", one item per line, up to 30 items). Every new visit of that service gets a copy, and so do upcoming visits that don't have one yet. Visits that already have a checklist keep it, so editing a template never rewrites history. Names match ignoring capitals and spaces. Saving an empty list removes the template.
- **Ticking items.** The owner and the crew member a visit is assigned to tick items off on the visit; the app records who ticked it and when. Nobody else can see or tick them, and checklists stop being editable on cancelled visits.
- **Photos.** Owners and the assigned crew member add before, after or other photos with an optional caption (200 characters, 30 photos per visit). The browser shrinks each photo to about 1600 px before uploading (usually 200 to 500 KB); the bucket refuses anything over 5 MB or that isn't a JPEG, PNG or WebP.
- **Private files.** Photos live in the private `job-photos` bucket under `business/visit/file`. Storage rules allow reading or uploading only for the owner and that visit's assigned crew member, and only the owner can delete. Photos are shown through short-lived signed links made as the signed-in user.
- **Customer page.** Owners see every photo from a customer's visits, newest first, with a link back to the visit.
- **Deleting.** Deleting a visit or customer also deletes their photo files so they stop using storage.
- **Storage limits (free Supabase plan).** 1 GB of file storage, shared with anything else in the project; the checklists page shows how much the photos use. When it's full uploads fail with a message and nothing else breaks. Remove old photos to make room, or move to the Supabase Pro plan.

## Reports decisions

- **Owner only.** Reports and CSV downloads are for the owner. Crew members get no Reports link, are sent to *My jobs* if they open the page, and the database functions behind it refuse them (so does the download address, with a 403).
- **Date range.** *Reports* shows this month so far by default. Pick *From* and *To*, or *This month*, *Last month*, *This year*, *Last year*. A range is five years at most.
- **What it shows:** booked and completed revenue, payments collected, expenses, profit, what's owed and what's overdue; revenue by month, customer and service; expenses by category; visits completed and scheduled per crew member (visits with nobody assigned are listed as *Unassigned*).
- **Revenue never double counts.** A visit counts once, at its price, or at its invoiced amount once it is on a sent invoice. Lines on sent invoices that aren't a booked visit (extras, or a visit that was cancelled) count once on the invoice's issue date. Cancelled visits and draft or cancelled invoices don't count. This is the same rule as the dashboard. *Booked* is everything not cancelled; *Completed* is only visits marked completed (and invoice extras).
- **Profit** is completed work minus expenses in the range. The cash basis (payments collected minus expenses) is shown beside it. *Owed* and *overdue* are as of today, whatever the range.
- **CSV export** (customers, jobs, invoices, expenses) for your accountant. Jobs, invoices and expenses use the chosen dates. Text that starts with `=`, `+`, `-` or `@` (or a tab) gets a leading apostrophe so a spreadsheet can't run it as a formula; money and counts are written as plain numbers. Downloads aren't cached by the browser.

## Request form decisions

- **A link for each business.** *Requests* shows your link (`/request/<64 random characters>`). It starts **off**; turn it on to share it. The page shows only your business's name and the form, nothing else about you. A wrong, off or old link shows a plain "This form isn't available".
- **The form:** name, phone, email, property address and what they need. Name and what they need are required, plus a phone or an email. Limits: 100 characters for the name, 40 phone, 200 email, 200 address, 1,500 for what they need. The database checks these too.
- **No account, nothing read back.** A visitor can send a request, and that's all. They can't read requests, links or any other data. A submission can only create a request for the business that owns the link.
- **Abuse protection without any paid service:** a hidden *honeypot* field (bots fill it in, people never see it; those submissions are quietly thrown away), and limits the database enforces: 3 requests per hour and 10 per day from one connection, 20 per hour and 100 per day per business, and no more than 500 unread requests. The connection is stored only as a one-way hash, never the address. A determined attacker can still send up to the per-business limit before it blocks everyone, so keep the link off when you aren't using it; *Make a new link* cuts off any link that leaks.
- **Everything is data.** Names and messages are escaped wherever they're shown (this page, and CSV exports neutralize formulas). Nothing a visitor types is ever treated as an instruction.
- **The inbox (owner only).** Each new request has *Add customer + quote*, *Add customer + book* or *Add customer*: it creates the customer from the request (what they asked for goes into the customer's service notes) and goes on to a new quote or booking. Converting twice returns the same customer. *Dismiss* hides a request you don't want; handled requests can be deleted. The menu shows how many are new.

## Monthly invoice decisions

- **Per customer.** On a customer's page, tick *Bill monthly*. Their completed visits are then billed together, one invoice a month, instead of one per visit.
- **A button, not a scheduler.** On the *Invoices* page, pick a month (this month or one of the last three) and press *Generate invoices*. Nothing runs by itself, so there's nothing to pay for or set up. Do it on the 1st for the month that just ended, or any time to bill so far.
- **What goes on the invoice:** that customer's visits in that month that are *completed*, have a price above $0, and aren't already on a live invoice. One line per visit ("Mowing (Tue, Oct 6)"), a note "Services for October 2026", issued today and due in 14 days. It's made as a **draft** so you can review it, then send it like any invoice.
- **Never bills a visit twice.** A visit can be on only one live invoice (a database rule), and the generator only picks visits that aren't on one. Pressing the button twice, or two people pressing it at once, makes no duplicates. If that customer's draft for the month already exists, visits completed since are **added to it** instead of making a second invoice. Once an invoice is sent it is never changed: a late visit gets its own new invoice. Cancelling an invoice frees its visits for the next run.
- **Auto-invoice** (a draft per completed visit) skips monthly customers, so their visits wait for the monthly invoice.
- Works with manual payments and without Stripe, like every invoice. Owner only.

## Time tracking decisions

- **Clock in and out.** On a visit, crew tap *Clock in* and later *Clock out* (the owner has the same buttons on a visit's page). The app records the start, the end and so the duration. A crew member can only clock in to visits **assigned to them** (the owner can clock in to any visit), and not to a visit that is already completed or cancelled.
- **One open entry at a time, per person.** Clocking in somewhere else while still clocked in is refused with a message naming the visit to clock out of first; tapping *Clock in* twice on the same visit changes nothing. The database enforces this too, so a double tap or two phones can't create two.
- **Crew see almost nothing.** A crew member sees only their own "clocked in since" time on the visit. They cannot read any time entries, hours worth or revenue per hour: the table is owner-only and everything they do is a function.
- **The Time page (owner only).** Hours per visit, per customer and per crew member for a date range, with each visit's and customer's worth and **revenue per hour**. Hours belong to the visit's date. Revenue per hour is per labor hour (two people working an hour each is two hours), using the visit's price, or its invoiced amount once it is on a sent invoice (the same rule as the dashboard and reports). Entries still clocked in aren't counted as hours yet and are flagged.
- **Fixing mistakes.** The owner can edit an entry's start and end (for example to close one someone forgot), and **must add a note** saying why. Times are entered on the business's clock and can't be in the future, end before they start, be over 24 hours, or overlap that person's other entries. The page shows "edited" and the note. The owner can also delete an entry. Deleting a visit deletes its entries; removing a crew member keeps their hours under their name.

## Today and the clock

- **Built for speed in the field.** Today is the first tab for everyone. At the top: the workday clock. Then the visit you're on, or the next one on your route, as a big card with the address (tap for directions), access notes and **one big button**. Then the rest of the day in route order, each with its own *Clock in*, and what's done folded away.
- **Workday clock (for payroll):** *Start my day* and *End my day* (which asks once, so a stray tap can't end someone's day). Ending the day also clocks you out of a job you forgot (the job stays open).
- **One tap per job:** *Clock in* starts your workday if it isn't started, clocks you in, and marks the visit in progress. *Clock out & finish* clocks out and marks it completed. *Clock out, not finished* is for a visit you're coming back to. The separate *Start job* and *Mark completed* buttons are gone from crew cards; *Could not service* and notes stay.
- **Who can clock in where:** a crew member only on visits assigned to them, the owner on any visit; one job clock and one workday per person at a time. Tapping twice changes nothing.
- **Forgotten clocks don't get anyone stuck.** A clock left running more than 16 hours is closed the next time that person clocks in: a workday at their last job clock-out, a job a minute after it started. Both are flagged on the Time page so the owner can set the real times (with a required note).
- **The owner sees who's working:** on Today, *Crew right now* lists each person: on the clock since when, and which visit they're at. The Time page adds *Workday hours (for payroll)* per person and a list of workdays to fix.
- Workdays are owner-only to read; crew only ever see their own clock. Nothing crosses businesses.

## Welcome screen and customer account decisions

- **Welcome screen.** Signed-out visitors to the site's home page choose who they are: *I run a landscaping business* (create a business account or sign in), *I'm on a crew* (sign in; new crew use their invite link) or *I'm a customer* (sign in; new customers use their invite link). Signed-in people go straight to their own place: owners and crew to the dashboard, customers to their account.
- **Customers join by invite only.** On a customer's page, *Invite to their account* makes a one-time link (valid 14 days, shown once, only its hash is stored), the same way crew are invited. Nobody can sign up and claim to be someone's customer. *New invite link* replaces a lost one; *Remove access* (or *Cancel invite*) turns it off, and their login then shows nothing.
- **One kind of login.** A login is either an owner/crew member of a business or one customer, never both. The database refuses a customer login joining a business, and a business login accepting a customer invite.
- **What a customer sees** (*/portal*): their upcoming visits and the last 90 days (date, service, status), quotes that were sent to them (never drafts) with their lines, invoices that were sent to them (never drafts or cancelled) with a *Pay* button to the existing pay page, and what they owe. They never see internal notes, gate codes, crew, other customers, expenses, reports or anything else; a customer login isn't a business member, so row-level security gives it nothing, and everything it sees comes from functions that only return that customer's own records.
- **Answering a quote.** *Accept* tells you they said yes (the quote shows "Customer said yes") and you then book it with the usual *They said yes* form to choose the dates and crew. *Decline* marks the quote declined. Each quote can be answered once, and not after it expires.
- **Asking for more work.** The customer's *Ask for more work* box drops a request into your Requests inbox, linked to that customer (up to 5 a day).
- Crew and owner logins are unchanged.

## Done for the season

- **Inactive means paused.** Marking a customer inactive (the *Done for the season* button on their page, or *Status* on Edit) takes their **upcoming repeat visits** off the schedule: visits today or later that haven't started (scheduled, assigned or weather delay). One-time jobs (a fall cleanup), visits under way or done, and past visits all stay. Their repeat services show *Paused for the season*, and no visits are added while they're paused (finishing a kept visit doesn't add one either).
- **Start again** (or *Status: Active*) brings the visits back **on their regular day**: same weekday and how-often as before, from the next one on or after today, after any of that service's visits that were kept (so a visit done today isn't doubled), and never before the service's first visit. Every-2-weeks services keep their own weeks, a regular day changed with *Also move their later visits* is kept, and so is the crew member. The usual 6 upcoming visits are added.
- **Booking while inactive:** one-time jobs book as normal. A repeat service books but waits (no visits) until they're marked active, and the booking goes back to the customer's page with a note saying so.
- **Stopped stays stopped.** A service stopped with *Stop* (for good) doesn't come back when the customer is marked active.
- **The database keeps it true.** A service's `paused` flag is set by the database from the customer's status on every insert and update, so no booking, accepted quote or hand-made API call can give an inactive customer repeat visits, and an owner can't unpause one directly. Pausing and resuming happen in a database trigger when the status changes, so the button and the Edit form behave the same. Only the owner can change a customer's status (row-level security), and it only ever touches that customer's own services in their own business.
- **Customers already inactive** when this was added were paused the same way: their upcoming repeat visits were removed.

## Search, Help and customer FAQ

- **One search box, owner only.** It sits at the top of every owner screen (in the sidebar on a computer, under the top bar on a phone). It finds the owner's own **customers** (name, phone, street, email), **visits** (customer, street, phone or service; the ones closest to today first), **quotes** (number like `Q-1001`, title, prospect or customer), **invoices** (number like `#1001` or `invoice 1001`, or the customer) and **requests** (name, phone, street, email or what they asked for), plus matching **Help** questions. Tap a result to open it.
- **How it matches:** at least 2 characters, up to 100. Capitals don't matter and what you type is matched as plain text (`%` and `_` aren't wildcards). Phone numbers match on their digits, but only when what you typed looks like a phone number, so "123 Main St" doesn't match every phone with 123 in it. Up to 20 customers and 10–12 of each other kind are shown.
- **Who can search:** only the owner, and only in their own business. It's one database function (`search_everything`) that checks the caller owns the business and filters every table by it; crew members, customers and signed-out visitors are refused, and crew are sent to their jobs if they open the search page. Crew don't get a search box: they only have their own jobs.
- **Help page** (*More → Help*, and *Help* next to *Sign out* on every screen): short answers to "how do I…?" (clocking in and out, the workday, booking, moving a rain day, invoices and payment apps, quotes, crew, routes, putting the app on a phone), written into the app (`src/lib/help.ts`). Crew see only their own topics, never anything about prices or money. A link to a question (`/help#clock-out`) opens it.
- **Ask a question.** The top of the Help page has an *Ask a question* box: type it in your own words ("how do I delay a client?", "my guy forgot to clock out") and the best one to three answers show right under it as you type. It understands everyday words for the app's terms (client → customer, delay/push/postpone → move, bill → invoice, estimate → quote, worker/guy → crew, payroll/hours → workday and Time), ignores filler words, and counts rarer words more (`src/lib/help-answer.ts`, with extra per-topic keywords there). It runs on the phone with no outside service: nothing typed is sent or saved. If nothing fits it says so plainly instead of guessing, and before anything is typed it shows common questions to tap. The search box at the top of owner screens uses the same answers when a search matches no Help question word for word. `/help?q=…` opens the page with a question already asked.
- **Customer FAQ** (*Settings → Customer FAQ*): the owner writes their own questions and answers (rain days, how to pay, what's included…), with common questions one tap away; the owner always writes the answer. Up to 50, 200 characters per question and 2,000 per answer, in the order the owner sets with the arrows. Customers see them on their account page and on the business's request form while its link is on, shown as plain text.
- **FAQ isolation:** the `faq_items` table is owner-only (read and write, through row-level security; the order is set only by the database). Customers read their own landscaper's questions through `portal_faq`, and request-form visitors through `public_request_faq`, which only answers for a link that's switched on. Crew have no access, and nothing crosses businesses.

## Menu and name decisions

- **Five sections instead of a long list, most-used first.** Owners see *Today*, *Schedule*, *Customers*, *Money* and *More*. Each section owns its pages, so the right one stays highlighted anywhere in the app, and its pages show as a short row of buttons at the top (Customers: All customers, Requests; Schedule: Calendar, My jobs, Weather; Money: Quotes, Invoices, Expenses, Reports; More: Crew, Time, Settings, Help). *Money* and *More* open a page of big, described buttons for new users. Crew see only *Today* and *My jobs*.
- **Phone tab bar.** On phones the five sections sit in a tab bar along the bottom (thumb reach, with icons); the new-request count shows on *Customers*.
- **The app's name lives in one file**, `src/lib/brand.ts` (`APP_NAME`, `APP_SHORT_NAME` for under the phone icon, `APP_TAGLINE`). Page titles, sign-in screens, the installed-app name and invite messages all use it.

## Moving visits decisions

- **Move on any visit** (owner): tap *Move*, pick *Today*, *Tomorrow*, a day this week, or any date (from 30 days back, to record work done earlier, up to a year ahead), and tap *Move to …*. Each moved visit gets a note ("Moved from Mon, Oct 26"); a visit marked *Unable to complete* goes back to *Scheduled*. Completed and cancelled visits never move.
- **Their regular day changing?** Tick *Also move their later visits by the same number of days*: every upcoming visit of that service shifts by the same number of days (finished ones stay), new visits keep coming on the new weekday, and that weekday becomes the customer's *Preferred day* (Sunday excepted). Picking two visits of the same service shifts the rest only once.
- **Several at once:** on a day's page, tick the visits (completed ones can't be ticked), pick the day and tap *Move N visits*. The box stays folded until you tick something; on a phone a floating button brings you back to it. You land on the new day with a summary.
- **Owner's Today:** each stop that hasn't started has a *Move* link (and *Not today? Move* on the next stop), which opens the visit with the move box ready.
- **Telling customers:** after a move, each customer who agreed to texts gets a *Text …* button with a ready-made message ("Hi John, it's EcoScape. We've moved your mowing to Wednesday, October 28. Thanks!"). It opens your phone's Messages app; the app sends nothing itself.
- One database function, `move_visits`, does it all in one step and checks the caller owns the business; crew can't move visits (they use *Could not service*). *Move a day's jobs* on the Weather page still moves a whole rain day at once.

## Deploying to a hosted Supabase project

1. Create a project at supabase.com. Then link it and apply the schema:
   ```bash
   npx supabase link --project-ref <your-project-ref>
   npx supabase db push
   ```
2. In the dashboard, under **Authentication → URL Configuration**, set **Site URL** to your app's URL (e.g. `https://app.example.com`). Add `https://app.example.com/**` to **Redirect URLs**.
3. Under **Authentication → Emails → Confirm signup**, replace the link in the template with the one from `supabase/templates/confirm-signup.html`:
   ```html
   <a href="{{ .SiteURL }}/auth/confirm?token_hash={{ .TokenHash }}&type=email&next=/">Confirm my email</a>
   ```
   The default template also works, but only if the link is opened in the same browser that signed up. The token-hash link works on any device.
4. Optionally, under **Authentication → Providers → Email** (password settings), set the minimum password length to 8 to match the app.
5. Set `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` on your host, e.g. Vercel. Both come from **Project Settings → API**.

## Hosting on Netlify (free, business use allowed)

The repo has a `netlify.toml` (app in `ecoscape-ops/`, Node 22). Netlify's Next.js adapter is automatic.

1. Sign up at netlify.com with your GitHub account (free plan). *Add new project → Import an existing project → GitHub →* pick this repo. The settings come from `netlify.toml`.
2. Before the first deploy, add environment variables `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_URL` and `NEXT_PUBLIC_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY` (same values as on Vercel), then deploy.
3. In Supabase, *Authentication → URL Configuration*: set **Site URL** to the Netlify address (or your own domain) and add `https://<that address>/**` to **Redirect URLs**. Sign-up confirmation and invite emails link there.
4. **Email for a public app:** Supabase's built-in email sends only a few emails an hour, which isn't enough once strangers sign up. Under *Project Settings → Authentication → SMTP*, connect a free email sender (for example Resend or Brevo) using your own domain.
5. Once Netlify works, delete the Vercel project (or leave it) and point your domain at Netlify.

## How tenant isolation works

The rules live in `supabase/migrations/` (foundation and scheduling):

- `businesses` is the tenant. `business_members` links auth users to a business with a role.
- Every tenant-owned table has a non-null `business_id`, and **row-level security is on for every table**. Policies only allow rows whose `business_id` is one of the caller's businesses. The lookup uses `private.member_business_ids()` / `private.owner_business_ids()`, which live in a schema that isn't exposed through the API.
- Clients can't write memberships at all. A business and its owner membership are created together, either by a trigger when someone signs up with a `business_name`, or by the `create_business()` function for a signed-in user who has none yet.
- A row's `business_id` can't be changed once set. The anonymous role has no access to any tenant table.
- **Crew members have no direct table access at all.** RLS gives them nothing on customers, service plans or jobs. Everything they see or do goes through `crew_*` database functions that only ever touch jobs assigned to the caller, and that never return prices. Crew members see only their own crew and membership records.
- Rows that point at other tenant rows use composite `(id, business_id)` foreign keys. A service plan or visit can only reference a customer or plan in the same business, even though foreign-key checks bypass RLS.
- **Weather texts:** who can be texted is decided by the database, never by the browser, and a rain delay's texts are visible only to the owner of that business.
- **Invoices and payments:** owners can read their business's invoices, but every change goes through database functions that enforce the rules; nobody writes invoices, lines or payments directly. A customer's pay link shows only that one invoice and that business's payment options. Payment options are owner-only too: only the owner reads and changes them (through a database function that checks their formats), and a pay link only ever shows its own business's.
- **Routes:** the shop location and every customer's location are written only by the owner, through database functions (owners can't even set a customer's location columns directly). Owners read and change any route in their business; crew members get no table access and reach only their own route through `crew_route` / `crew_route_settings`, and can reorder or restart only that one. A route's order can only contain that route's own visits on that day.
- **Quotes:** owners read their quotes and lines; every change goes through database functions that enforce the rules, and crew members and signed-out visitors have no access at all.
- The app code filters by business too, but only for clarity. The database enforces isolation even against hand-crafted API calls, and `tests/db/tenant-isolation.test.ts` checks this.

**Checklist for each new tenant table** (jobs, invoices, …):
1. Add `business_id uuid not null references businesses(id) on delete cascade`.
2. Reference other tenant rows with composite keys: `foreign key (x_id, business_id) references x (id, business_id)`.
3. `enable row level security`, with policies based on `private.owner_business_ids()` / `private.member_business_ids()`.
4. Add explicit `grant`s for `authenticated` and none for `anon`.
5. Add the `prevent_business_id_change` trigger.
6. Add cross-business tests to `tests/db/`.
